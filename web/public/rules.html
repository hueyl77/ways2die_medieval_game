Rules of play

A Thousand Ways to Die in Medieval

Honest trades, unfortunate accidents.

  • 3–8 players
  • 45–75 minutes
  • Ages 12+

A medieval village of honest tradespeople, a blacksmith, an innkeeper, a jeweler, a thief. Everyone works, everyone earns, and everyone smiles across the market square. But the neighbors keep having accidents, bees, geese, falling roof tiles, the occasional trebuchet, and nobody ever saw who arranged them. Earn more than anyone, survive the year, and never let them find out which trade is yours.

1 Overview

Every coin is a clue

Each player secretly runs one of eight trades. Gold is earned by trades, not by players, on a public board, so every coin you bank is also evidence about who you are.

The game is played in rounds. Each round, every player places one card face-down in front of every seat at the table, including their own. Piles are shuffled before they're flipped, so no card is ever traceable to the player who placed it. Attacks wound (and every wound you still carry at the end costs a coin), heals mend, protects shield, job cards bank gold, and nobody can prove anything about anyone.

Object of the game

Be alive at the end of the year with the richest trade among the survivors.

2 Components

What's in the box

  • 8 trade envelopes, each containing 36 cards: 1 role card (with a distinct back) · 27 job cards · 2 Heals · 1 Protect · 3 signature cards unique to that trade (§8).
  • The Mishap deck: 36 Attack cards, 24 Mishaps (1 wound, every one a different accident) and 12 Calamities (2 wounds). See §15.
  • Gold board with 8 trade tracks and 8 gold markers.
  • 12 crest cards (player identities, used for wills).
  • Townsfolk marker for an automated seat (§10).
  • 20 wound tokens for wounds beyond a card's first.
  • Crier token (first player) · round marker · calendar strip.

The eight trades: Blacksmith, Farmer, Thief, Innkeeper, City Guard, Jeweler, Apothecary, Miller.

Public knowledge

Every envelope has the same structure, and the exact contents of all eight are printed in §8. Every seat also holds exactly three Mishaps and one Calamity, but which ones is anyone's guess. Counting what has and hasn't been revealed is a core skill of the game.

3 Setup

Dealing out the village

  1. Place the gold board in the center; set all 8 gold markers to 0.
  2. Fill the table to at least four seats. With three players, add one Townsfolk seat (§10): an empty chair with the Townsfolk marker. Seats = players + Townsfolk.
  3. Consult the table for your seat count; place the round marker on the calendar.
  4. Shuffle all 8 envelopes and deal one to every seat, players and Townsfolk alike. Return the rest to the box unopened: nobody knows which trades are absent.
  5. Privately open your envelope. Memorize your role card and return it to the envelope; keep the envelope face-down in front of you all game. Set aside job cards above your "job cards kept" number, face-down to the box. Take everything else into your hand. Then shuffle the Mishaps and deal three face-down to every seat, shuffle the Calamities and deal one to every seat, and return the rest to the box unseen; add yours to your hand. Prepare any Townsfolk seat as described in §10.
  6. Each player takes a crest marker. The matching crest cards go face-down in a stack in the center, the succession, touched only when someone dies. Townsfolk take a crest too, so a ghost may name one as heir.
  7. Give the Crier token to the player who most recently sold something.

4 Playing a round

4.1 Gossip

Talk while you place. Accuse, deny, promise, and lie as much as you like. Nothing said is binding, and you may never show cards or your role. There is no separate discussion step: the round is open for placement from the start, and it ends when everyone has placed.

4.2 Placement

Each player places exactly one card face-down in front of every seat, including their own (and one on each grave, §9). Placements are made openly: people see where you place, never what. Ghosts may haunt now (§9.3), and each Townsfolk's tender deals its cards (§10).

4.3 Shuffle

Each player picks up the pile in front of them and shuffles it without looking at the faces; a Townsfolk's tender shuffles its pile. From this moment, no card can be traced to its placer.

4.4 Reveal & resolve

Starting with the Crier and going clockwise, each pile is flipped face-up and fully resolved (§5) before the next is flipped. Grave piles flip last; their cards have no effect (§9.2).

4.5 Cleanup

Resolved cards go face-up to a shared discard in the center, the town square. Wounds, scoring cards, and pending cards stay where they are. Advance the round marker, apply any season-end event if the Turning Year variant is on (§7), and pass the Crier token left.

5 Resolving a pile

Order of effects

  1. Voids. Apply everything that cancels cards: a Protect voids all Attacks in the pile (Turning Year variant, Winter: each Protect voids only one); Night Patrol, Curfew and Bad Batch apply as written; a Broken Door discards a Protect before anything else. Card text always beats season rules.
  2. Wounds. Each surviving Attack card stays face-up in front of the pile's owner as a wound. A card that deals more than one wound (a Calamity, a Sneak Attack, anything sharpened by a Grindstone) adds a wound token beside it for each extra wound.
  3. Heals. Each Heal removes one wound from the pile's owner, a token first, then a card (to the town square). Attacks and Heals in the same pile net out: a Heal can save you from a killing blow revealed alongside it. Heals beyond your wounds are wasted; you cannot bank health.
  4. Death check. If the owner's wounds equal their death number, they die, finish resolving the pile, then hold the funeral (§9.1).
  5. Gold. A pile visited by a Tax Collector earns nothing this round (see §8). Otherwise Alms is judged first (see §8), on the board as it stood. Then each job card banks 1 gold to its trade's track, no matter whose pile it was in, and no matter who placed it. Apply any bonus (Market Fair, in the Turning Year variant), then signature gold effects.
  6. Words. Truth cards (Inquest, Appraisal) are answered immediately, out loud, truthfully. This is a binding rule of the game, not a suggestion.
  7. Pending. Cards with "next round" effects stay face-up on the pile as reminders, then discard when spent.

Wounds are cards

Health needs no tracker: landed Attacks sit in front of you as wounds (with a token for any second wound), heals take them away, and everyone can always see exactly how close to death everyone else is.

6 Gold

Trades get rich, not people

  • Gold belongs to trades, not players. The track is public; its owner is not.
  • Job cards bank regardless of which pile they appear in. Placing your own job card in front of an enemy is a perfectly safe placement, it pays you either way.
  • Scoring cards (Blackmail) stay face-up in front of the player they hit and apply to that player's own trade at final scoring, whatever trade that turns out to be.
  • Wounds cost gold. Every wound still in front of you at the final reveal scores −1 for your trade. An attack that doesn't kill still pays, and a heal is worth a coin to whoever receives it.
  • A dead player's trade is revealed and its track locks: no gains, no losses, no victory, and it is ignored when a card targets the "richest" or "poorest" track.
  • Tracks of absent trades are normal targets. Nobody knows which tracks those are, a climbing track might be a Gleaning, an Alms… or a real neighbor getting rich.

7 The calendar

The calendar, and the optional variants

In the standard game every round plays the same. The seasons are names on the calendar and nothing more. The rules below are an optional variant, The Turning Year, for tables that want the year to escalate; when it's on, season rules apply to every round of that season. Six-round games (4–5 seats) play the full year; four-round games (6–8 seats) start at Harvest.

Spring
Full year 1–2

No special rule. Learn faces. Tell your first lies.

Harvest

Market Fair: every job card banks +1 gold.

Season's end, the Reeve's Tax: every track tied for richest loses 2.

Winter
Full year 5–6 · Four rounds 3–4

The Hungry Winter: each Protect voids only one Attack.

Two more optional leader rules, also off by default, for tables that find the leader runs away: the Reeve's tithe: after every round each track pays 1 gold per 8 it holds (a strongboxed track pays nothing); and the Reckoning: at the start of the final round, every living seat holding the richest trade opens its envelope and plays the last round unmasked.

8 The trades

Signature cards

Every envelope also holds one **Alms** card: *printed for its own trade. Alms is judged first in the gold step, on the board as it stood before this round's wares: if that trade is clearly last or second-to-last among the trades still in play, ties do nothing, so at the start of the game nobody qualifies, it gains 4 gold.* Alms is the village's catch-up, and like a signature card it proves its trade is at the table. Every envelope also holds one **Tax Collector**: *the crown's man visits this pile, every coin that would be earned from the cards in it this round (wares, Alms, and gold-gaining signature cards) goes to the crown instead.* Drop it where you expect a rival's wares to land; a Protect or a Heal in the same pile still works. Every envelope holds three signature cards, the only copies in the game. Playing one is powerful, and it's also a confession: it proves your trade is at the table, though never which chair it sits in. Silver Dagger, Sneak Attack, and Broken Door count as Attacks for every rule and truth card.

The arms dealer

  • GoldIron Strongbox: The pile's owner sets this beside any track. For the rest of the game that track ignores every effect that would take gold from it, taxes, thefts, and scoring penalties alike.

🌾 Farmer

The provider

  • HealHearty Stew: The pile's owner heals 2.
  • GoldBumper Crop: The Farmer track gains 1; the pile's owner heals 1.

🗡 Thief

The criminal

  • GoldCutpurse: Move 2 gold from the richest track other than the Thief track to the Thief track.
  • ScoringBlackmail: Stays in front of the pile's owner. At the final count their trade loses 1 gold and the Thief track gains 1.

🍺 Innkeeper

The host

  • RevealStrong Ale: The pile's owner is drunk: they must spread their entire hand face-up on the table for everyone to see while the Crier counts slowly to five, then gather it up.
  • HealA Round on the House: Every wounded player heals 1; the Innkeeper track gains 1 per player served (max 3).
  • VoidBad Batch: All Heals revealed in this pile this round are voided.

🛡 City Guard

The law

  • ShieldNight Patrol: Void up to two Attacks in this pile and one in each neighboring pile; the City Guard track gains 1 per Attack voided (max 2).
  • PendingCurfew: Next round, every Attack revealed anywhere is voided; the City Guard track gains 1 per Attack stopped (max 3).
  • TruthInquest: The pile's owner must truthfully answer: "Did you place any Attack this round?" The City Guard track gains 1 for the inquiry.

The wealth broker

  • GoldKing's Commission: The Jeweler track gains 2.
  • TruthAppraisal: The pile's owner must truthfully announce how many job cards remain in their hand.

⚗ Apothecary

The poisoner-physician

  • HealPanacea: The pile's owner heals 2, and it cures a Slow Poison on this pile without being spent.
  • PendingSlow Poison: Stays on the pile: at the end of next round the owner takes 1 wound, voided if any Heal is revealed in this pile first (that Heal is spent as the antidote). Protects do not stop it; it is not an Attack.

The cheat

  • GoldMiller's Toll: The Miller track gains 1 for every two job cards revealed in this pile (max 3). Everyone's grain comes through the mill.

9 Death & the dead

The village keeps its ghosts

9.1 The funeral

  1. Open your envelope and reveal your role card. Your trade's track locks (§6).
  2. Your remaining hand is shuffled face-down onto your seat: this starts your grave pool.
  3. Seal your will. Take the succession stack, browse it privately, and choose the crest of one living seat, player or Townsfolk, your secret heir. Seal exactly one crest inside your envelope: choose in secret, seal in view, so the table sees one card go in without seeing its face. Return the stack to the center. Keep your envelope in front of you; it can never be changed, and your heir is not told. If no living player's crest remains, you die without a will.

9.2 The grave

Your seat stays in the game. Every living player must still place one card on each grave every round, the grave is the village's only dump, and what people throw away there is public information. Grave piles are shuffled (by the ghost) and revealed like any other, but their cards have no effect. After the reveal they join that grave's pool, face-down.

9.3 The haunt

  • Talk freely in every Gossip phase. You have an agenda; nobody should trust you, and that's the point.
  • Haunt. Each Placement phase you may take one card from your grave pool (you may examine your pool privately at any time) and place it face-down into any living player's pile. The table sees which pile the grave visited, never what it sent. Haunted cards resolve normally. Job cards of your own locked trade bank nothing; anyone else's bank as usual.

9.4 Winning from beyond

When the wills are opened at game end, a ghost whose sealed crest names the winner shares the victory. Each crest exists once, so each heir can serve only one ghost, dying early means first pick of heirs. If your heir dies, your legacy dies with them.

10 Townsfolk

The stranger at the table

A Townsfolk is a seat run by chance instead of a player, a stranger with a trade of its own. Use one whenever there are only three players; you may also add up to two at any size for a more chaotic village.

Setup

Deal the Townsfolk an envelope like anyone else. The player on its left is its tender. Without looking at any faces, the tender slides the role card out (role cards have a distinct back) and returns it to the envelope, then shuffles all the remaining cards, no trimming, together with the seat's three Attack cards into a face-down deck at the seat. Nobody knows the stranger's trade.

Each round

During Placement, the tender deals one card from the deck, face-down and unseen, into every pile at the table, the Townsfolk's own pile included. The tender shuffles the Townsfolk's pile before the reveal. Townsfolk do not gossip and never haunt.

Its cards

They resolve exactly like anyone's: its job cards bank to its hidden trade, its Attacks wound, its signature cards fire. When a card revealed in a Townsfolk's pile calls for a choice, the Crier chooses. Truth cards and Strong Ale revealed in a Townsfolk's pile have no effect.

Its death

A Townsfolk that takes enough wounds dies like anyone: reveal its role, lock its track, and shuffle its remaining deck face-down onto its seat as a grave the living must still feed each round. It seals no will and does not haunt.

It can win

A Townsfolk competes like any other seat at the final count, if the stranger out-earns the village, the stranger takes the year.

11 Game end

The final reveal

  1. The final reveal. After the last round of Winter, all living players open their envelopes and show their roles at once.
  2. Settle accounts. Apply each player's scoring cards (+/−) and −1 per wound to their own trade's track.
  3. Crown the winner. The living seat, player or bot, whose trade's track is highest wins. Ties go to the fewest wounds; if still tied, the winners share it.
  4. Open the wills. A ghost whose crest names the winner rises to share the win.

Last one standing. If a round ends with only one villager still alive, the year ends at once: the survivor wins outright, and a ghost whose will names them shares the win.

If everyone dies, the village stands empty and nobody wins.

12 Table rules

The laws of the village

  • Never show cards. Not your hand, not your role, not "just one." The only reveals are funerals, the final reveal, and a Strong Ale.
  • Truth cards are binding. Answers to Inquest and Appraisal must be honest, and a drunk shows their whole hand. Everything else may be a lie.
  • No binding deals. Promise anything; deliver whatever you like.
  • Shuffle blind. Never look at faces while shuffling a pile, and anyone may ask for a pile's card count before it's flipped.

13 Rulings

Frequently asked

Can I place an Attack in front of myself?
Yes. Any card may go in any pile.
Who is "adjacent"?
Your seated neighbors, alive or dead.
Richest/poorest ties?
The pile's owner chooses among tied tracks. The Reeve's Tax has no owner: every tied track pays.
Slow Poison vs. Panacea?
Any Heal revealed in the pile is spent to cure a pending poison; Panacea cures it and still heals.
Can a ghost haunt a grave, or skip haunting?
No graves; only living players' piles. Skipping is allowed.
How do I heal a Calamity?
A Heal removes its wound token first; the card stays as one wound until healed again.
What does "cannot be voided by a Protect" cover?
Protect cards. Night Patrol and Curfew still stop a Silver Dagger or Sneak Attack.
What does a drunk have to show?
Every card in their hand, face-up, for a slow count of five. The role card stays in the envelope; wounds and scoring cards are already public.
A pile's count looks wrong before the flip?
Count it openly, a haunted pile legitimately runs one card heavy.
Do the Townsfolk's cards count when I'm counting Attacks still at large?
Yes. Treat the stranger's deck as unknown: everything in it is possible until it's revealed.
Can a Townsfolk's trade end up the richest track?
Yes, and until it dies, nobody can prove which track is the stranger's. If it is still the richest at the final count, the stranger wins.

14 First year

Advice for new villagers

  • Your own pile is a decoder ring. You know the one card you placed in front of yourself. Subtract it from your pile's reveal, and what's left is exactly what the table did to you.
  • Count everything. The envelope contents are public. Every Attack that hasn't appeared is still in someone's hand.
  • Nobody can kill alone, but every hit pays. Five wounds of attacks against a defended player will not be enough on their own; murders take partners, and partners take gossip. Meanwhile every wound you leave on a rival is a coin off their score, so aim your attacks at the trade you suspect is winning rather than dumping them on a grave.
  • Earning is confessing. Every coin on your track sharpens the table's guess. The winner is usually the player who got rich slowest.

15 The mishap deck

A thousand ways to get hurt

Every Attack card in the game is a different way to get hurt. Mishaps deal 1 wound; Calamities deal 2. All of them are Attacks for every rule, void, and truth card. Each seat draws three Mishaps and one Calamity at setup, so the table always knows how many accidents are waiting, never which.
