# Gameplay example — a narrated round (3–4 minutes)

Captured from a seeded 5-seat game (`scripts/record-demo.ts --seed 28`), narrated over real footage. The narrator plays the Jeweler; the other seats are bots but are treated as villagers in the narration.

## Cold open (0:00–0:15)
*Five villagers, six rounds, and nobody knows who's who. I'm the Jeweler — but as far as the table knows, I could be anyone.*

## Reading the hand (0:15–0:50)
Shot: the hand, hovering cards one by one.
*Eighteen wares, two heals, a protect, three accidents, a calamity, one Alms for the Jeweler, one Tax Collector, and my three signature cards: King's Commission, Appraisal, Paste Gems. Every wares card I play is gold on the Jeweler track — and a clue.*

## Placement (0:50–1:30)
Shot: dragging cards to seats, then Lock in.
*Marta gets a wares card — harmless, and it pays me anyway. Bram gets the goose: one wound if nothing stops it. I keep a Protect in front of myself, because the first round is when everyone tests the water. Lock in.*

## The reveal (1:30–2:40)
Shot: scenes stepping with Next; coins flying; the ledger.
*Every pile is shuffled before it flips. Marta's pile: four wares and a hidden rake — somebody tried. Bram's: the goose lands; Bram takes a wound. Mine: two attacks — and my Protect voids them both. The gold board ticks up as the wares flip. The ledger shows the Jeweler at 4, Innkeeper at 4... and nobody can say who placed what.*

## Talking it over (2:40–3:05)
Shot: chat panel, a couple of lines.
*Between rounds the table talks. "I healed you, Bram." "No one healed me." Both true, both unprovable. That's the game.*

## Later: a death and a will (3:05–3:35)
Shot: funeral modal for a bot? (staged: the narrator's seat dies), heir picked.
*By round four Odo is gone — envelope open, he was the Thief all along. His will names an heir in secret. From here he haunts: one card a round from beyond the grave.*

## The final count (3:35–4:00)
Shot: end screen.
*Round six. Every envelope opens. Wounds cost a coin each, Alms and Blackmail settle, and the richest survivor wins the year. This time, the Innkeeper — and Odo, whose will named her, rises to share it. Next year, I keep my head down.*
